### The rule worth reading twice

**Reuse matches the implementing class, not the match capability.** Every numeric, date and boolean type is unanalyzed, so match analysis reports all of them as matching whole values — exactly what a string type reports. A reuse rule written against the capability would point a text field's `_exact` companion at whichever `plong` the schema happened to declare first. The schema would accept it, and the failure would arrive at index time against real documents rather than in the editor.

`_exact`
`plong`

The generated catalog cannot settle it either: its `PRIMITIVE` trait covers numeric, boolean, string, date, UUID and enum types together, because it exists to decide `omitNorms` rather than to separate these. So the check is `StrField` by name. A pure test declares `plong` and `pdate` ahead of `string` and asserts `string` still wins; a fixture test covers the same trap end to end.

`PRIMITIVE`
`omitNorms`
`StrField`
`plong`
`pdate`
`string`
`string`

Already-capable is the mirror of that, and is granularity rather than class: a keyword-tokenized *text* field holds the whole input as one term just as a `string` field does, and has equally nothing to gain. Both are silent.

`string`

### The refactor

The two companions differ in exactly two places — what counts as already capable, and which declared types may be reused — and agree everywhere else. The shared rules moved to `SolrCompanions`, the shared PSI insertion to `SolrAddCompanionIntention`, leaving each companion as its policy.

`SolrCompanions`
`SolrAddCompanionIntention`

This is not tidying. The companion-name rule and the generated-name collision rule are the kind that get fixed once and left broken in the copy, and a user would meet the difference before anyone reading the code did. The prefix half's 17 tests were re-run unchanged across the refactor, before any exact behaviour was added.

### The generated type

Copied from Solr's own `_default` configset rather than invented — it is the one declaration in a schema that almost never varies, and matching it means a reader recognises the line instead of checking it. `sortMissingLast` puts documents lacking the field at the end of a sort rather than the start; `docValues` is what a facet or sort actually reads.

`_default`
`sortMissingLast`
`docValues`
